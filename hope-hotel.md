# HOPE HOTEL
### A Practical, Fundable, Dignified Solution to Urban Homelessness
*Conceived by Stephane, Montreal, May 22, 2026*

---

## The Problem in Plain Numbers

**Montreal:** Approximately 4,690 people experiencing visible homelessness (2022 provincial survey — likely an undercount by 2026 given continued increases).

**Canada-wide:** The 2024 national Point-in-Time count found homelessness up 79% since 2018. The crisis is not stabilizing. It is growing.

**What we currently spend to do nothing well:**

| What we pay for | Cost per person per year |
|----------------|--------------------------|
| Federal prison (Canada) | $114,000–$150,000 |
| Homeless person with mental illness (Montreal/Toronto/Vancouver) | $50,000+ in emergency services |
| ER visits, ambulances, police calls for unhoused individuals | $16,000+ per person |
| **Hope Hotel — full service, dignified housing** | **~$10,500** |

We are spending more money to keep people on the street than it would cost to house them. This is not a resource problem. It is a priority problem.

---

## The Model: What Works

**Housing First** — give people stable housing unconditionally, then provide support. Not the other way around.

This is not theory. It has been proven:

- **Houston, Texas:** 63% reduction in homelessness since 2012. Over 30,000 people housed. Achieved without spending city money beyond existing federal allocations — by coordinating resources smarter.
- **Finland:** Virtually eliminated rough sleeping by converting shelters into small private apartments with on-site support. Residents are tenants with rights, not patients in a program.

The evidence is settled. Housing First works. What is missing is a replicable, privately fundable model that any city can deploy without waiting for government action.

That is Hope Hotel.

## What Hope Hotel Is

A 200-unit residential building. Modern. Dignified. Indistinguishable from market housing on the outside.

Not a shelter. Not a dormitory. A place people actually want to live while they get back on their feet.

**Each unit:** Small private studio. Your own door. Your own key. Basic furnished. Clean.

**Shared amenities:**
- Small clinic with a nurse on shift
- Community kitchen and subsidized dining room (no one turned away hungry — emergency food always available)
- Laundry
- Library and quiet reading space
- Classroom (AA meetings, literacy programs, whatever residents need)
- Event space (movie nights, karaoke, community dinners)
- Business center: computers, printer, phone
- Gym
- Small store: affordable essentials, clothing, haircuts

**Philosophy:** Everything is available. Nothing is mandatory. Residents are adults.

---

## Who Gets In

**Referral only.** No advertising. No self-referral.

Entry through: social workers, hospital discharge coordinators, shelter workers, community health nurses.

This protects the mission and ensures units go to those with genuine need — not to people seeking cheaper rent.

A small independent intake committee (including community workers and a resident representative) reviews referrals and manages the waitlist.

## The Math: How Many People Can One Hotel Help?

This is the part that surprises people.

A 200-unit hotel is not a static solution for 200 people. It is a **throughput system**.

If the average resident stabilizes and transitions to independent housing in **4–6 months**, one hotel can serve **400–600 people per year**.

**Montreal example:**
- Estimated 4,690+ people experiencing homelessness
- One Hope Hotel: 200 units, ~500 people helped per year
- Five Hope Hotels: ~2,500 people helped per year — more than half the visible homeless population cycled through dignified housing and support within 12 months
- Over 10 years: tens of thousands of lives changed, with compounding effect as people who stabilize stop cycling through emergency services

This is not solving homelessness forever. It is making a serious, measurable dent — affordably, replicably, and without waiting for a perfect political solution.

**One-time construction or acquisition cost:** Converting an existing hotel runs $8–15 million depending on city. New construction: $20–30 million.

**Endowment model:** Raise $40–50 million once. Invest conservatively. Use returns to fund operations permanently. No ongoing fundraising required. The hotel runs forever.

**Capital Reserve Fund:** 5% of all contract and earned revenue is automatically directed to a Capital Reserve Fund, held in a separate interest-bearing account managed by the board, reserved exclusively for major structural repairs and lifecycle replacements (roofing, HVAC, elevators — costs that occur every 10–15 years and run $500K–$2M). It cannot be raided for operating expenses.

## The Anti-Corruption Constitution
### In Plain Language

This document governs Hope Hotel. It cannot be changed without a two-thirds majority vote that must include resident board members.

**1. The Mission Is Locked**
Hope Hotel exists to provide dignified transitional housing to people experiencing homelessness, referred through trusted community channels. It cannot be repurposed, rebranded, or redirected to serve a different population without dissolving the organization and returning assets to a similar mission-aligned charity.

**2. Residents Have Real Power**
The governing board always includes at least two current or recently transitioned residents with full voting rights. They can raise concerns, block decisions, and participate in hiring the building manager. Symbolic participation is not enough. Real power or nothing.

**3. No One Person Controls the Money**
All financial decisions above $10,000 require sign-off from at least two board members from different backgrounds. Accounts are audited annually by an independent firm. Results are published publicly.

**4. The Funder Has No Special Authority**
Whoever funds Hope Hotel — individual, foundation, or government — does not get operational control in exchange for money. Funding is a gift to the mission, not a purchase of influence. This is non-negotiable and written into the founding charter.

**5. Staff Are Not Saviors**
Staff are hired to support residents, not manage them. Any staff member found to be infantilizing, controlling, or disrespecting resident autonomy is subject to immediate review. People with lived experience of homelessness or addiction are actively recruited for staff roles.

**6. The Building Manager Can Be Fired by Residents**
If a majority of residents sign a petition of no confidence in the building manager, the board must convene a review within 30 days. Residents are not powerless in their own home.

**7. Harm Reduction, Not Punishment**
Substance use behind a closed door is a health matter, not a disciplinary one. Naloxone is available everywhere. No one is evicted for using substances. Eviction is reserved for violence or behaviour that makes other residents unsafe — and even then, only after documented attempts to find another solution.

**8. Integration, Not Isolation**
Hope Hotel is located in a mixed neighborhood, not segregated in an industrial zone. Residents are encouraged to participate in the surrounding community. The building does not look or feel like an institution from the outside.

**9. The Boat Rule**
No individual who works for, governs, or funds Hope Hotel may personally benefit financially from its operation beyond their stated salary or grant. This includes contracts with related companies, real estate transactions involving the property, or any arrangement that converts charitable resources into personal wealth. Violation triggers immediate removal and public disclosure.

**10. The Capital Reserve Is Untouchable**
A Capital Reserve Fund is maintained at all times, funded by directing 5% of all contract and earned revenue into a separate interest-bearing account. It exists exclusively for major structural repairs and lifecycle replacements. It cannot be used for operating expenses under any circumstances. Spending from it requires a full board vote with resident member participation. Its balance is published in the annual public accounts.

**11. Failure Is Allowed. Drift Is Not.**
Things will go wrong. Residents will have crises. Staff will make mistakes. That is acceptable and human. What is not acceptable is the slow drift away from dignity and toward institutionalization — more rules, more surveillance, more control — without residents pushing back. The constitution exists to make drift structurally difficult. If Hope Hotel ever starts to feel like a shelter with better furniture, this document should be read aloud at the next board meeting.
